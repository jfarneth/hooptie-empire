Hooptie Empire · feature spec

Vehicle rarity

Four trim grades on every car the feed deals. Rarity is visible — a spoiler, a lift, a stripe, a bar of neon under the sill — and it raises what the car retails for without raising what the seller wants for it. Condition stays the thing you have to guess at.

The ladder

Frequency assumes the base feed — one listing every 22 s, no auction contacts. A fully scouted feed runs 3.3× faster, which puts a legendary at roughly one every three hours.

The bottom strip is the size a car is actually drawn at on a small lot (~34 px) and on a driveway (~60 px). That is the test the trim has to pass: the spoiler and the lift are silhouette changes so they survive it, the stripe reads as a value break, and the underglow is the only thing that reads as colour at 34 px — which is why it is reserved for one car in a thousand.

Both axes are live

Rarity × condition a legendary beater is still a beater

Rarity multiplies value; condition multiplies value. They are independent, and the art says so — the underglow does not stop the paint going chalky or the rust blooming. A 20% legendary is worth less than a 90% common, and that is the read a player should get from the picture before they open anything.

What it is worth

Gross margin as a share of retail, at each store's mid-band ask. Margin is 1 − 0.74 × ask ÷ rarity: the ask is drawn against base trim, so the whole premium lands on your side of the deal.
Store Ask (mid) Common Rare Epic Legendary

Weighted across the 90/9/0.9/0.1 population the economy-wide lift is small at the bottom and large at the top: +4.5% profit per car at a curbstone lot, +13.6% at a Valmont store. Thin margins are the reason — the same ten points of value are worth far more where there were only seven to begin with. That inversion is the interesting part of the feature and the part that needs a 64-seed harness run before it ships.

How it lands in the code

One value seam

Rarity multiplies conditionFreeValue and nothing else. Retail, wholesale, recon cost, the finance window, the forced-sale haircut and the traffic reference all derive from it, so every one of them prices rarity correctly with no second call site.

One ask seam

spawnListing prices the ask against a base-trim copy of the car. That single line is the whole feature: the seller does not charge for the spoiler, the retail buyer pays for it.

Nothing is hidden

Rarity is on the listing at full strength — no appraisal noise, no band. You can see a lift kit. Condition stays private, which keeps the honest line the game already draws between what an operator could know and what they could not.

No new sprites

Baking rarity would take the sprite matrix from 81 frames to 324 and needs Blender, which a normal checkout does not have. Trim is an SVG overlay composited over whatever drew the car, so it works over the sprite and the vector fallback alike.

Save v10 → v11

Car.rarity is save data. The migration backfills common on every car and every listing — which is exactly the game as it exists today, so a live save notices nothing.

The open question

A legendary lives 150 s on the feed. At one in a thousand, the best thing in the game will usually turn up while nobody is watching. Longer lifetimes for the top two grades is the proposed answer.
